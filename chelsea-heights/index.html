Chelsea Heights — Chelsea's inland half

Chelsea Heights sits directly east of Chelsea, separated from the coastal suburb by the Frankston railway line. The suburb's character is shaped by its inland position: no beach access, a broader street grid, newer housing estates mixed with established residential, and a commercial profile centred around the Wells Road and Argyle Avenue corridors rather than a traditional shopping strip. The Frankston–Dandenong Road runs along the suburb's eastern boundary, giving it highway access that the coastal suburbs don't have.

The light industrial and trade services zone along Wells Road accommodates the kind of businesses that need space and road access over retail visibility: builders' merchants, trade supplies, automotive services, light manufacturing and warehousing. This gives Chelsea Heights a dual character — residential streets that generate service demand on one side, and a commercial/industrial strip that generates B2B and trade supply demand on the other.

The Chelsea Heights residential and trades market

Chelsea Heights is more affordable than the coastal suburbs, which shapes the businesses that thrive here. Value-for-money trades, automotive services, home maintenance and practical professional services — accountants, mortgage brokers, insurance advisers — find a customer base that is cost-conscious and loyal. The residential density is high enough to generate consistent demand across all these categories.

For tradies, Chelsea Heights is the inland base that lets you service both directions. To the west, the coastal Chelsea, Bonbeach and Carrum catchment. To the east, the Carrum Downs and Frankston North residential suburbs. A Chelsea Heights tradie who builds location pages for both directions creates a service area that covers a substantial portion of outer south-eastern Melbourne from a central, well-connected base.
