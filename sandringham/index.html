Sandringham — the village on the bay

Sandringham sits 16 kilometres south of the Melbourne CBD, between Hampton to the north and Black Rock to the south. It's one of the original bay-side holiday destinations — residents of Melbourne were taking the train to Sandringham for beach days and weekends from the 1880s, and that railway heritage (the Sandringham line terminates here) shaped the suburb's compact, walkable character.

The most striking feature of Sandringham's foreshore is its Yacht Club harbour. The Sandringham Yacht Club operates one of the largest marinas on Port Phillip Bay — 365 berths — and the harbour sits over the hull of a deliberately scuttled World War I submarine, adding a layer of genuine local history beneath the boats. Directly opposite the Sandringham Hotel, perched on the clifftop above the beach, the 1926 band rotunda is surrounded by palm trees and looks out over the water — the kind of landmark that becomes a mental postcard for anyone who lives here.

The shopping precinct sits close to the railway station, with Station Street as the main dining and café strip. It's compact rather than sprawling — Sandringham doesn't try to be Church Street Brighton, and that's part of its appeal. The suburb maintains a genuine village feel that most bayside communities with its proximity to the CBD would have long since lost.
