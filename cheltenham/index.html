Cheltenham — southern Melbourne's commercial anchor

Cheltenham sits 18 kilometres south-east of Melbourne's CBD, at the southern end of the City of Bayside and into the northern reaches of the City of Kingston. The suburb's western section — towards the golf courses, the parks and Port Phillip Bay — falls under Bayside council, while the eastern section from the Frankston railway line and Charman Road falls under Kingston. That dual-council character shapes the suburb's identity as a commercial hub for two LGAs rather than just one.

The most significant commercial landmark is Westfield Southland on Nepean Highway — one of the largest shopping centres in Australia by floor area, at over 129,000 square metres and approximately 400 retailers including Myer, David Jones and Harris Scarfe. Southland draws shoppers from across the southern suburbs, making Cheltenham a retail destination that its immediate residential population alone couldn't sustain. The Southland railway station, which finally opened in 2017 after being proposed more than 130 years earlier, added public transport access to this retail activity and increased the suburb's accessibility from the broader Melbourne network.

Along Charman Road, next to the railway station, the Cheltenham Pioneers Cemetery — established in October 1864 — stands as a reminder of how long this area has been settled. The Bay Road precinct provides an additional commercial strip serving the immediate residential community with cafes, specialty shops and services that draw from the surrounding streets rather than from across the city. Cheltenham is a suburb with genuine commercial depth, not just a Southland carpark.

Local web design in a competitive Cheltenham market
