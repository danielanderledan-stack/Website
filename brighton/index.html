Brighton's business landscape

Brighton sits 11 kilometres from the Melbourne CBD and is home to around 23,000 residents. The suburb is defined by two retail strips that run the length of its character: Church Street — Melbourne's go-to destination for high-end fashion, beauty, homewares and dining, anchored by the Dendy cinema — and Bay Street in North Brighton, the older, more eclectic strip where independent cafes and fashion boutiques have operated for generations alongside the kinds of specialty shops that never survive in a mall.

The suburb's other calling card is the bathing boxes at Dendy Street Beach. These 82 privately owned timber boxes, painted in stripes and patterns, have lined the foreshore since the 1860s — they're one of Melbourne's most recognisable icons and draw visitors from across the city. Brighton Town Hall on Carpenter Street and the Italianate Kamesburgh mansion on North Road add to a suburb that takes its heritage seriously.

Brighton is part of the City of Bayside, which means its business community shares council infrastructure and the same local government area as Black Rock, Sandringham, Hampton and the other Bayside suburbs. For local SEO purposes, that shared council boundary matters — it's one of the signals Google uses to define a local service area.

Why Brighton businesses need better websites

The customer on Church Street expects quality. They check reviews before they book. They look at the website on their phone on the walk from the tram stop. If the site loads slowly, looks outdated, doesn't have a working online booking or contact form, or doesn't show up when they search "hair salon Brighton" or "electrician Brighton Melbourne" — they move on to the next result, and they don't come back.

Brighton has high foot traffic and strong word of mouth, but neither of those things helps a business when someone new to the area — a renter, a recent buyer, someone who just moved from the other side of Melbourne — is searching from scratch. That first Google result, that first impression on a mobile screen, is where local businesses win or lose customers they never know they lost.
