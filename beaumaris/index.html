Beaumaris — coast, cliffs and community

Beaumaris sits 20 kilometres south-east of the Melbourne CBD, between Black Rock to the north and Cheltenham to the south, with Port Phillip Bay forming its entire western boundary. The suburb is defined by its foreshore — specifically, the stretch from Table Rock Point to Charman Road, where the Beaumaris Cliffs rise above the bay. Those cliffs are recognised as a site of international scientific significance: the exposed rock contains marine and terrestrial fossils that have been studied by researchers for decades, making Beaumaris's coastline genuinely remarkable in ways that most visitors don't know.

At Ricketts Point, the foreshore gives way to a 115-hectare Marine Sanctuary established under Victorian legislation in 2002. Crystal-clear shallow water, sandstone reefs, sea caves and rockpools make it one of the bay's most accessible natural environments. The Royal Melbourne Golf Club, which established its course on the Beaumaris border in 1901, is another institution that shapes the suburb's character — it sits on the landward side and draws members and visitors from across Melbourne.

The Concourse is Beaumaris's main retail and hospitality strip, running across North, East and South Concourse. It's compact and walkable, with a mix of cafes — including Ginger Fox, which has become a local institution — specialty shops, and service businesses. The Lobster Cave, one of Melbourne's better-known seafood restaurants, draws diners from across the city. It's a strip that rewards local businesses with loyal regulars and a flow of visitors who make the drive specifically because of the foreshore.
