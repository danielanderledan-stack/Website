Black Rock — a suburb with a story

Black Rock sits between Sandringham to the north and Beaumaris to the south, 18 kilometres south-east of the Melbourne CBD. The suburb takes its name from Black Rock House, a grand homestead built in 1856 by colonial politician Charles Hotson Ebden — one of the earliest substantial residences in the bay area, now a heritage landmark in the City of Bayside.

The suburb's most distinctive feature is Half Moon Bay. The bay has been a centre for sailing since the 1890s, and in 1919 the Black Rock Yacht Club was established as a branch of the Brighton club. In 1926, the hull of the colonial naval vessel HMAS Cerberus was deliberately sunk to form a breakwater protecting the boat haven — that rusting iron hulk, visible at low tide, is one of the most photographed and recognisable sights on the Bayside foreshore. The existing seawall was built by Depression-era sustenance workers, another layer of local history that most visitors don't know.

The local shopping precinct sits at the intersection of Bluff Road, Beach Road and Balcombe Road — a small, walkable cluster of cafes, services and specialty shops that serves the surrounding residential streets without trying to compete with Hampton Street or Church Street. That's Black Rock's character in a sentence: quality over volume, local over national, community over crowd.
